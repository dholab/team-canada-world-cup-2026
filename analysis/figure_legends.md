# Figure legends

Punctuation note. These legends avoid em dashes, colons, and semicolons per author preference. Times are reported as local wall-clock time for each host city, which spans four time zones over the tournament.

---

## Figure 1

**Figure 1. Respiratory-virus detections in team-space air samples across the 2026 FIFA World Cup.** Air was sampled continuously in Team Canada congregate spaces across five host cities from 3 June to 4 July 2026, and every cartridge was tested for SARS-CoV-2, influenza A, influenza B, and respiratory syncytial virus (RSV). All cities are shown on a single continuous local-time axis, so both within-city gaps and inter-city travel gaps appear to true elapsed-time scale. Rows are the four target viruses. Each rectangle is one cartridge tested for one virus, drawn to that cartridge's exact sampling window, so box width reflects the real duration of each collection session. Rooms are merged within each city in this figure, so each box summarises all rooms sampled in that session. A box is blue if the virus was detected in any room, with fill mapped to the lowest cycle-threshold (Ct) value across rooms (a lower Ct, shown in deeper blue, indicates more viral nucleic acid). A box is white if no room detected the virus but at least one room returned a valid negative result. A box is grey only if every room in that session was invalid, meaning the assay's internal sample processing control did not amplify (no such session occurred in this study). An asterisk beneath the axis marks a session in which one or more rooms were invalid even though other rooms returned valid results, so the reader can see where room coverage was incomplete. A sample counted as a detection if the instrument reported any Ct value for a target, regardless of its qualitative call. The per-room data are explorable in the interactive version, and the Houston episode is shown by room in Figure 2.

## Figure 2

**Figure 2. Per-room detections during the Houston sampling period.** Sampling in Houston ran from the evening of 30 June to 4 July 2026 across four team spaces, being the physiotherapy room, the meal room, the equipment room, and a team-occupied hallway. Rows group the four target viruses within each room, and the horizontal axis is local wall-clock time. Each rectangle is one cartridge tested for one virus, drawn to its exact sampling window. White boxes are valid negative samples, grey boxes are invalid runs (in which the internal sample processing control did not amplify), and blue boxes are detections, with fill mapped to the Ct value (deeper blue indicates a lower Ct and more viral nucleic acid). On 1 July, SARS-CoV-2 was detected in the meal room, the physiotherapy room, and the hallway, and influenza A in the equipment room. No virus was detected on 2 July. SARS-CoV-2 returned on 3 to 4 July at lower Ct, most strongly in the meal room, indicating more airborne virus. The two invalid runs fell on 1 July. An interactive version allows hovering over any interval to read the room, virus, exact sampling window, duration, Ct value, qualitative call, and control status.
